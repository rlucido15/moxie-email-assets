General

What is a mortgage?

+

A mortgage is a loan used to buy a home or property, where the home serves as collateral until the loan is fully repaid.

How does a mortgage work?

+

A mortgage allows you to borrow money from a lender to buy a home, then repay it (plus interest) in monthly installments over a set term, typically 15 to 30 years.

What is included in my monthly mortgage payment?

+

Your payment may include:

  • Principal
  • Interest
  • Property taxes
  • Homeowners insurance
  • Mortgage insurance (if applicable)

What are closing costs?

+

Closing costs are one-time fees paid at closing that typically range from 2% – 5% of the purchase price. They include lender fees, title insurance, appraisal fees, and more.

Can I pay off my mortgage early?

+

Yes, you can do so in several forms including paying extra principal monthly, requesting a recast, or by requesting a payoff. Some products may carry prepayment penalties, so be sure to check your loan terms first.

What happens if I miss a mortgage payment?

+

If you miss a mortgage payment, you may incur late fees, damage your credit score, and face foreclosure if the issue remains unresolved for an extended period of time.

What is an escrow account?

+

An escrow account holds a sum of funds set aside to pay property taxes and insurance on your behalf. While not always mandatory, escrow accounts are commonly required for mortgages, particularly when a down payment is less than 20% or with certain loan types like FHA or USDA loans.

What is equity in a home?

+

Equity is the portion of your home that you own outright. Your equity increases as you pay your mortgage* and as property increases in value.

*While monthly payments for most loans include principal and interest, there are loan types for which the monthly payment only includes interest and therefore will not build your equity position with each payment.

What is a loan estimate?

+

A loan estimate is a standardized, three-page document that lenders provide to borrowers after they apply for a mortgage. It outlines the key terms and projected costs of the loan, including interest rate, monthly payments, and closing costs.

What are origination fees?

+

Origination fees are charged by the lender for processing your loan application — usually around 0.5% to 1% of the loan amount.

What is a rate lock and should I use one?

+

A rate lock guarantees your interest rate for a set period, protecting you from rate increases. A standard rate lock is 30 days; however, Moxie has the ability to lock your rate for up to 360 days.

How long does it take to close a mortgage?

+

Most lenders typically require 30–45 days to close a mortgage; however Moxie can close in as little as 10 days, making our buyers' offers more attractive to sellers.

Can I get a mortgage with a co-signer?

+

Yes, a co-signer with strong credit and income can help you qualify, but they will be equally responsible for the loan.

Is it safe to give my Social Security number to a lender?

+

Yes, if you're working with a reputable lender. They need your SSN to access your full credit report, verify your identity, and provide an accurate loan estimate.

What is a prepayment penalty?

+

A prepayment penalty is a fee charged when a borrower pays off all or part of their mortgage early, typically within the first few years of the loan. This can include:

  • Paying off the loan in full ahead of schedule
  • Refinancing with another lender
  • Making extra large payments that significantly reduce the principal

What mortgage products allow prepayment penalties?

+

Not all mortgage loans can legally include prepayment penalties. Here's a breakdown:

  • Not allowed: Conventional loans (owner-occupied), FHA loans, VA loans, USDA loans
  • Allowed: Non-QM loans, DSCR/investor loans, hard money loans

How do I qualify for a mortgage?

+

What is mortgage pre-approval?

+

Pre-approval means a loan officer has reviewed your credit, assets, and income to issue a preliminary approval that, absent other changes in circumstances, your assets can cover the required cash-to-close, your income satisfies debt-to-income requirements, and your credit meets loan requirements.

What's the difference between prequalification and preapproval?

+

Pre-qualification is an estimate of what you could afford that is lacking one or multiple required criteria for a full preapproval — such as proof of funds, verification of employment/income, or satisfactory review of credit.

Can I get a mortgage after bankruptcy or foreclosure?

+

Yes, it is possible to get a mortgage after bankruptcy or foreclosure, but there will likely be waiting periods and stricter requirements. Waiting periods:

  • VA loans: Typically, 2 years after a Chapter 7 discharge.
  • Conventional loans: Generally, 4 years after a Chapter 7 discharge or 2 years after a Chapter 13 discharge.
  • Non-QM loans: May offer shorter waiting periods.

What is a fixed-rate vs. adjustable-rate mortgage?

+

A fixed-rate mortgage has the same interest rate for the life of the loan. An ARM has a lower initial rate that adjusts periodically based on market conditions.

What is an FHA loan?

+

An FHA loan is a type of mortgage that is insured by the Federal Housing Administration (FHA). It's a popular option for homebuyers who may not meet the stricter requirements of conventional loans, especially first-time homebuyers or those with lower credit scores or limited savings.

What is a VA loan?

+

A VA loan is a mortgage loan that is guaranteed by the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs (VA). This program helps eligible veterans, active-duty service members, reservists, and certain surviving spouses purchase, build, or refinance a home. While VA loans are issued by private lenders, the VA guarantees a portion of the loan, reducing the risk for the lender and offering favorable terms for borrowers.

What is a USDA loan?

+

A USDA loan is a government-backed mortgage offered by the U.S. Department of Agriculture (USDA) designed to help low-to-moderate income individuals and families in rural and suburban areas purchase, build, or renovate homes. These loans often feature no down payment requirement and competitive interest rates, making homeownership more accessible, especially for those in less populated areas.

What is a jumbo loan?

+

A jumbo loan is a mortgage that exceeds the standard conforming loan limits set by the Federal Housing Finance Agency (FHFA). These limits are updated annually by the FHFA. In 2026, a loan exceeding $832,750 is considered a jumbo loan in most counties; however, in areas deemed to be "high-cost," the conforming loan limit can be higher — up to $1,249,125.

What is a Non-QM loan?

+

A Non-QM (Non-Qualified Mortgage) loan is a type of mortgage that doesn't meet the traditional lending criteria set by the CFPB (Consumer Financial Protection Bureau) for "Qualified Mortgages." These loans are designed for borrowers who don't fit into the conventional mortgage mold, but are still creditworthy.

Can I get a mortgage for a fixer-upper?

+

Yes, FHA 203(k) and Fannie Mae HomeStyle loans allow you to roll renovation costs into the mortgage.

Which mortgage loan is best for first-time homebuyers?

+

An FHA loan is often an attractive option for first-time buyers due to their low down payment options (as low as 3.5%) and more flexible credit requirements.

Can I switch loan types later?

+

Yes, you can refinance to a different loan type subject to loan approval. Examples:

  • Refinance from an ARM to a fixed-rate loan
  • Refinance from an FHA to a conventional loan

What are common types of Non-QM loans?

+

Common examples of Non-QM loans include:

  • Bank statement loans – Qualify based on 12–24 months of bank deposits instead of W-2s or tax returns.
  • Asset depletion loans – Use liquid assets to qualify for income.
  • Interest-only loans – Lower monthly payments for a set term by paying only interest.
  • DSCR loans (debt-service coverage ratio) – Popular with investors; qualification is based on property cash flow, not personal income.
  • Foreign national loans – For non-U.S. citizens purchasing property in the U.S.

Are Non-QM loans safe?

+

Yes, Non-QM loans are legal and regulated. They're simply non-traditional, and lenders often use manual underwriting to assess risk. They are not subprime loans; many borrowers have good credit but unique income or asset situations. Moxie offers a complete range of Non-QM products. Contact Moxie for more information today.

Are interest rates higher on Non-QM loans?

+

Generally, yes. Non-QM loans carry slightly higher interest rates than conventional loans to account for the additional risk or flexibility offered by the program.

What is a DSCR loan?

+

A DSCR loan is a type of non-QM mortgage designed for real estate investors. Instead of using your personal income, the lender qualifies the loan based on the cash flow of the investment property you're buying or refinancing. This makes it ideal for investors who want to scale without being constrained by their personal debt-to-income ratio.

What is the Debt-Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR) and how is it used?

+

DSCR is a key metric for investment loans. It compares a property's monthly rental income to its monthly mortgage payment. Moxie offers DSCR loans from a .75 ratio and higher, meaning the property generates enough income to cover 75% of the monthly mortgage payment.

Formula: DSCR = Gross Rental Income ÷ Monthly Mortgage Payment

  • A DSCR of 1.0 means the property breaks even.
  • A DSCR above 1.0 means the property earns more than it costs.

Can I use DSCR loans to buy multifamily properties?

+

Yes, DSCR investment loans can be used on:

  • Single-family homes
  • Duplexes
  • Triplexes
  • Fourplexes
  • In some circumstances, 5–8 unit options

Rates and Terms

What is mortgage insurance?

+

Mortgage insurance protects the lender if you default and is a GSE requirement for mortgages with a down payment less than 20%.

What factors affect mortgage rates?

+

Several factors influence your mortgage rate, including:

  • Credit score
  • Down payment amount
  • Loan type and term
  • Debt-to-income ratio (DTI)
  • Current market conditions
  • Property location and type

How can I get a lower rate?

+

You can get a lower rate by working with a direct-to-wholesale lender like Moxie Mortgage. Unlike traditional retail lenders, Moxie cuts out the middlemen and overhead that tend to increase rates for borrowers. Additionally, you can improve your credit score, improve your debt-to-income ratio, increase your down payment, and consider options such as temporary or permanent rate buydowns.

What is a discount point?

+

A discount point is a fee you can pay at closing to reduce your interest rate over the term of the loan.

What's the typical loan term?

+

Most mortgages in the United States span 15 or 30 years. Moxie has a range of options including 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, and 40 years. Mortgages with shorter terms have higher monthly payments, but lower total interest paid relative to longer term options.

What's the APR and how is it different from the interest rate?

+

Interest rate reflects only the cost of borrowing. APR (Annual Percentage Rate) includes the interest rate plus fees like loan origination, mortgage insurance, and closing costs — giving a more complete picture of the total loan cost.

How does the Federal Reserve impact mortgage rates?

+

The Federal Reserve does not set mortgage rates, but it influences them by changing the federal funds rate, which influences the bond market. The bond market — in particular the 10 Year Treasury — is the foundation of the 30 Year Fixed Rate mortgage in the United States.

Can mortgage rates change after I'm pre-approved?

+

Yes, a preapproval gives an initial indication of your eligibility, but your rate is not locked until you go under contract and formally lock it with the lender.

What is a float-down option?

+

A float-down option allows you to lock in a rate but drop to a lower rate if the market improves during your lock period. It often comes with added fees or conditions.

Can I renegotiate my rate after locking?

+

No, unless you have a float-down option. Otherwise, once locked, your rate is fixed through the lock period even if rates drop.

Do I have to pay mortgage insurance?

+

If you're putting down less than 20% on a conventional loan, you'll likely pay Private Mortgage Insurance (PMI). FHA loans also require mortgage insurance premiums (MIP).

Is private mortgage insurance (PMI) required for Non-QM loans?

+

Typically, no. Most Non-QM loans do not require mortgage insurance — even if the down payment is less than 20%.

Do student loans affect my mortgage approval?

+

Yes, lenders factor your minimum monthly payment into your debt-to-income (DTI) ratio — even if the loan is in deferment.

What is a hard credit inquiry?

+

A hard inquiry (or hard pull) is a type of credit report that is required as part of a mortgage application. It may cause a small, temporary drop in your credit score — usually around 2 points.

What's the difference between a hard and soft credit pull?

+
  • Soft pull: Doesn't impact your credit score. While common for pre-qualifications or personal credit checks, soft pulls are not sufficient for mortgage approval.
  • Hard pull: Can slightly lower your score. Used for mortgage approvals and new credit applications.

How long does a hard inquiry affect my credit score?

+

Typically, a hard inquiry may impact your score minimally for up to 12 months, but it disappears from your credit report entirely after 24 months.

Will multiple inquiries hurt my score more?

+

Not necessarily — credit bureaus group mortgage inquiries made within a 45-day window (FICO) as one single inquiry.

Appraisal

What is a property appraisal?

+

A property appraisal is a professional, third-party assessment of a home's market value.

Why is an appraisal required for a mortgage?

+

Lenders use appraisals for third-party verification that the property value justifies the loan amount and loan-to-value ratio.

Who pays for the appraisal?

+

The buyer typically pays for the appraisal. It usually costs between $400–$800, depending on property size, location, and type.

What happens if the appraisal comes in low?

+

Assuming your contract has an appraisal contingency, you have several options if the appraisal comes in low.

  1. Renegotiate the purchase price
  2. Pay the difference in the form of a greater down payment
  3. Dispute the appraisal with a Reconsideration of Value (ROV)
  4. Cancel the contract

How long does an appraisal take?

+
  • Appraisal inspection: 60 minutes
  • Report delivery: 3–10 business days based on market demand and appraiser availability

What does the appraiser look for?

+

Appraisers evaluate the following when considering the market value of a property:

  • Property condition (interior & exterior)
  • Square footage
  • Number of bedrooms & bathrooms
  • Location
  • Comparable recent sales ("comps")
  • Upgrades, renovations, and lot size

What's the difference between an appraisal and a home inspection?

+
  • Appraisal: Estimates market value of a property for the lender
  • Inspection: Evaluates the physical condition of a property and seeks to identify any property issues for the buyer

Do renovations and upgrades impact the appraisal?

+

Yes, renovations to a property, in particular improvements to kitchens, bathrooms, flooring, roofs, HVAC, additional bedrooms, and finished basements often increase the value of a home.

What is a 1004 appraisal form?

+

A 1004 appraisal, also known as the Uniform Residential Appraisal Report (URAR), is the most commonly used full appraisal report for single-family homes. It's used to determine the market value of a property and is required for most home loans.

What is a 1007 appraisal form?

+

A 1007 appraisal is used to estimate the market rent of a property — especially for investment properties. It's typically required for:

  • DSCR loans
  • Investment property purchases or refinances
  • Loans using rental income to qualify
  • Conversion of primary residences into rental properties

Refinance

What is mortgage refinance?

+

A mortgage refinance is the process of replacing your current mortgage with a new one, often to get a lower interest rate, a different loan term, or to access equity.

What are the reasons to refinance?

+
  • Lower interest rates: When rates drop, refinancing can help you secure a lower rate, saving you money over the life of the loan.
  • Change loan terms: Refinancing to a shorter term, like moving from a 30-year to a 15-year mortgage, can help you pay off your home faster and save on interest.
  • Change interest rate types: Switch from an adjustable-rate mortgage (ARM) to a fixed-rate mortgage for more predictable monthly payments.
  • Pull equity out of your home: Depending on your equity, a cash-out refinance lets you pull equity out of the home tax-free.
  • Consolidate debt: Use your home equity to pay off high-interest debts like credit cards or student loans.

How do I know if refinancing will save me money?

+

If your current rate is more than 0.5%–1% higher than today's market rates, refinancing could be worth exploring. Contact Moxie Mortgage for a refinance assessment to see how much you could potentially save.

What are the requirements to refinance?

+
  1. Sufficient home equity
  2. Solid credit score (620+ for most lenders)
  3. Stable income and low debt-to-income ratio
  4. On-time payment history

Will I need another appraisal when refinancing?

+

In most cases, lenders require an appraisal to confirm the current market value of the property; however, FHA Streamline and VA IRRRL refinances generally do not require an appraisal.

Can I roll refinance closing costs into my loan?

+

Yes, often closing costs can be financed into the new loan, reducing your upfront out-of-pocket expenses.

What is a streamline refinance?

+

A streamline refinance is a simplified process available for current FHA or VA loan holders, which does not require an appraisal.

What are the costs of refinancing?

+

Closing costs for refinances can range from 2%–5% of the loan amount. Moxie Mortgage offers no-cost refinances for qualifying borrowers that purchased the same property with Moxie.
